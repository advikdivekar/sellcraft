# Build SellCraft AI — A World-Class, Futuristic AI Startup Website

You are a world-class creative developer, senior frontend engineer, motion designer, and product designer. Your job is to transform **SellCraft AI** into an exceptionally polished, futuristic, premium AI startup website that feels ready for a Y Combinator-backed company launch.

Do not produce a generic SaaS landing page. Build an immersive digital experience with sophisticated typography, cinematic scroll interactions, spatial zoom effects, premium product visualizations, fluid transitions, and excellent attention to detail.

The final website should be visually stunning, fast, responsive, accessible, and genuinely usable.

## 1. Understand the product first

SellCraft AI is an AI-powered content creation tool for online sellers, small businesses, handmade-product brands, Instagram stores, Etsy sellers, Shopify merchants, and other independent entrepreneurs.

The user provides:
- A product photograph.
- A few product facts, features, materials, pricing details, or other relevant information.
- Their target platform, such as Instagram, Facebook, Pinterest, Etsy, or Shopify.
- Their preferred tone, such as persuasive, emotional, luxury, playful, minimal, or straightforward.

SellCraft AI turns those inputs into a complete product marketing package:
- SEO-friendly product titles.
- Detailed product descriptions.
- Search keywords and tags.
- Social media captions.
- Platform-specific copy variations.
- Short-form video advertisement concepts, with a target duration of approximately 10 seconds.
- Optional voiceovers and narration through an interchangeable text-to-speech integration.

The core promise:

**One product photo. A few details. A complete marketing package.**

Design the whole website around this transformation. A visitor should understand the product within five seconds.

## 2. Overall visual direction

Create a premium, future-facing visual identity with the confidence and simplicity of an exceptional early-stage technology company.

Use the following creative direction:

- A sophisticated near-black and warm-white foundation, or a primarily warm-white canvas with deep charcoal typography.
- One distinctive, high-energy accent color, such as electric lime, vivid chartreuse, or a carefully selected acid-green tone.
- Beautiful typography with strong oversized headlines, tightly controlled line heights, and deliberate negative space.
- Large editorial compositions that alternate between spacious minimalism and immersive, full-width visual moments.
- Fine borders, subtle gradients, restrained glow effects, realistic shadows, and carefully layered depth.
- A consistent spacing system, precise alignment, and an excellent visual hierarchy.
- Beautiful product mockups that look like a real working product rather than decorative dashboard placeholders.

Avoid excessive gradients, random neon colors, glassmorphism everywhere, rounded-card overload, generic stock photos, emoji-based feature icons, and repetitive three-column feature sections.

Do not simply copy another company's website. Create an original visual identity for SellCraft AI.

## 3. Cinematic hero section

The first screen should feel extraordinary.

Create a full-viewport, meticulously composed hero with:

**Navigation**
- SellCraft AI wordmark.
- Product.
- How it works.
- Features.
- A restrained button for signing in or getting started.
- A mobile navigation menu that works properly.

The navigation may begin transparent and transition to a lightly blurred, solid background once the visitor scrolls.

**Hero headline**

Use this exact headline as the initial direction:

"From product photo
to ready-to-sell."

Make the typography enormous but elegant. Use intentional line breaks, tight spacing, and a subtle entrance animation. Highlight one phrase using the brand accent.

Supporting copy:

"Turn a single product photo into scroll-stopping ads, persuasive listings, and platform-ready content — all powered by AI."

Primary CTA: "Create your first listing"

Secondary CTA: "See how it works"

Include a small, understated note such as "Made for the people who sell."

**Hero visual**

Create an immersive central composition showing the SellCraft AI transformation.

A product photograph should appear as the source object. Around it, multiple beautifully designed output panels emerge naturally:

- A product listing with an SEO title.
- An Instagram caption.
- A set of product tags.
- A short video-ad storyboard.
- A platform-specific preview.

Use a combination of real image assets, CSS, SVG, and actual UI components. Make the composition look like real software, not a pile of generic floating cards.

The photograph is the source, while the output panels represent the completed marketing package.

Give the composition physical depth using layered transforms, subtle perspective, varying scales, and carefully controlled shadows.

Use a tasteful pointer-responsive parallax effect on desktop, while ensuring the experience remains stable for touchscreens and reduced-motion users.

The hero should be visually impressive without making the content difficult to read.

## 4. Make scrolling feel cinematic

This is one of the most important requirements.

Implement a carefully choreographed scroll experience using tools compatible with the project's existing stack.

As the visitor scrolls through the page, the website should feel as though they are moving through a carefully designed product world.

### Animation sequence A: Zoom into the product

Initially, the product photograph is relatively small and surrounded by the floating marketing outputs.

As the visitor scrolls:
- The source photograph smoothly scales up.
- Surrounding output panels move outward or fade away.
- The photograph becomes the focal point of the viewport.
- The camera-like perspective transitions seamlessly to the next scene.

Use scroll progress to control the transformation rather than triggering arbitrary animations at fixed scroll positions.

The transition should feel like a smooth camera push-in, not an abrupt element enlargement.

### Animation sequence B: Transform the product

Continue scrolling to transform the original image into its finished listing experience.

- The original photo shifts to one side.
- Product information appears alongside it.
- A title types or reveals itself with elegant timing.
- Keywords and description lines appear in sequence.
- A platform selector changes the appearance of the content preview.
- Small status indicators communicate the transformation without pretending that a real AI generation is running.

Keep every animation synchronized with the scroll position.

### Animation sequence C: Zoom out into the complete marketing package

As the visitor continues scrolling, smoothly pull back from the product preview.

The individual outputs arrange themselves into a coherent marketing package containing copy, tags, social content, and the short video-ad concept.

The wider scene should reveal how one input produces multiple useful deliverables.

Use motion to communicate the product's value rather than adding animation solely for decoration.

### Animation sequence D: Transition to the next section

Use a fluid scale, opacity, and position transition to move from the immersive scene into a clean editorial section.

The page should never feel like a collection of unrelated animation demos.

Transitions must be seamless, intentional, and performant. Avoid excessive scroll hijacking. Scrolling should remain natural, and all key content must remain accessible when animation is disabled.

## 5. Build a genuinely convincing interactive product demo

This should be the centerpiece of the website.

Create a section titled:

"One product. Every channel. Ready to sell."

Build an interactive, responsive product-generation interface that visitors can actually experiment with.

The demo should contain:

**Input area**
- Product image upload.
- Drag-and-drop support.
- A sample-product button that loads a bundled demonstration image.
- A product name or short description field.
- Additional product details.
- Target-platform selection.
- Tone selection.
- A prominent "Generate marketing package" button.

**Platform options**
- Instagram.
- Facebook.
- Pinterest.
- Etsy.
- Shopify.

**Tone options**
- Luxury.
- Persuasive.
- Emotional.
- Playful.
- Minimal.
- Professional.

**Generated output area**

Display a beautifully designed, tabbed output experience featuring:
- Product title.
- Product description.
- Search tags.
- Social caption.
- Video-ad concept.

Each tab must work and display a meaningful, relevant example.

Switching platforms should update the example layout and its associated sample content where appropriate. Switching tones should visibly change the sample copy when the generation logic supports it.

Include working copy buttons and useful feedback when content is copied.

The demo must not pretend that a backend AI call exists when it does not. If the real generation API is unavailable, provide an explicit demo mode with predefined or locally generated sample outputs.

Where the project already has a working API, connect the interface to it rather than replacing actual functionality with fake results.

Make the sample experience impressive enough that users can understand the value of the real product without signing up.

## 6. Showcase short-form video creation

Video creation is an important differentiator. Give it its own visually striking section.

Headline:

"Your next ad starts with a photo."

Create a premium, vertical-video-inspired showcase with a realistic phone or video preview.

Show a sample progression:

1. The original product image.
2. A cinematic product shot or animated scene.
3. A short promotional hook.
4. Product benefits appearing on screen.
5. A polished end frame featuring the product and CTA.

Make this a short, approximately 10-second video-ad concept.

Use a genuine video asset if one is available in the repository. Otherwise, create an animated storyboard preview with CSS, SVG, and existing image assets. Clearly distinguish a storyboard preview from an actual generated video.

Include an interactive play/pause button when using an animated preview. If an actual video asset is available, include working playback controls.

Show how the same product can be packaged for different social channels.

Do not build a fake video generator that claims to have rendered a downloadable video when no actual generation pipeline exists.

## 7. Add a premium "one input, many outputs" visualization

Create a distinctive, full-width section that visually demonstrates the SellCraft AI workflow.

The source product image occupies the center of the composition.

As the visitor scrolls into the section, animated paths or lines connect the source image to different outputs:

- Product listing.
- SEO keywords.
- Instagram post.
- Pinterest pin.
- Video-ad concept.
- Brand-specific caption.

Reveal the output previews in a carefully choreographed sequence.

The result should look like an elegant visual system rather than a flowchart or a standard feature grid.

Use SVG paths, CSS transforms, scroll-linked animations, and small motion accents. Do not introduce a massive particle effect or an unnecessary 3D engine merely for visual spectacle.

The visualization should explain the product without requiring lengthy explanatory text.

## 8. Design the remaining landing-page sections

Build a complete landing page with a clear, conversion-oriented narrative.

### Section 1: Hero
Introduce the value proposition and showcase the product.

### Section 2: The transformation
Use the cinematic zoom sequence to explain how a product photograph becomes a complete marketing package.

### Section 3: Interactive studio
Let visitors upload a sample image, select a platform and tone, and explore the generated outputs.

### Section 4: Made for every selling channel
Present Instagram, Facebook, Pinterest, Etsy, and Shopify as distinct content destinations.

When a channel is selected, update a real preview component instead of merely changing a highlight.

### Section 5: Video-ad studio
Show the short-form video concept and explain how product imagery can become a promotional creative.

### Section 6: Brand voice
Show how different writing tones change the same product's marketing. Use a few polished before-and-after examples.

### Section 7: How it works
Explain the process in three concise steps:

1. Upload your product.
2. Choose your style and platform.
3. Generate your marketing package.

Use clean typography and minimal visual clutter.

### Section 8: Final CTA
Use a visually confident closing section.

Headline:

"Your product deserves better marketing."

Supporting text:

"Create better listings, stronger product stories, and campaign-ready content from the photos you already have."

CTA: "Start creating"

### Section 9: Footer
Include the brand, relevant navigation links, and existing social or legal links if available. Never invent working destinations for links that have not been configured.

The website should feel complete, but avoid adding unnecessary sections just to make it longer.

## 9. Motion design and micro-interactions

Use motion throughout the experience, but establish a consistent motion language.

Implement the following where appropriate:

- Smooth scroll-triggered reveals.
- Scroll-linked zoom-in and zoom-out transitions.
- Subtle depth and perspective.
- Staggered text entrances.
- Carefully timed image reveals.
- Gentle floating product-output panels.
- Understated button hover states.
- Smooth tab and platform transitions.
- Subtle cursor-responsive movement on large desktop compositions.
- Animated connecting lines.
- Content transitions that preserve layout stability.
- Small feedback animations when copying content or completing an interaction.
- Sophisticated navigation transitions.
- Smooth modal and image-upload states.

Animation principles:
- Favor transform and opacity where possible.
- Avoid layout thrashing and expensive continuous effects.
- Keep motion timing consistent.
- Do not animate every element independently.
- Avoid constantly moving backgrounds.
- Avoid excessive spring overshoot, bouncing, spinning, and glowing.
- Support `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Ensure animations do not prevent users from reading the page or interacting with controls.
- Disable or substantially reduce pointer and scroll-linked effects on devices where they could harm usability.

The visual result should feel precise, confident, and exceptionally well executed.

## 10. Typography and layout

Typography is a major part of the design.

Use an elegant, modern sans-serif typeface available in the project or load an appropriate web font when the project supports it.

Use:
- Dramatic display typography for the main headline.
- Compact, purposeful headings for each section.
- Comfortable body text.
- Restrained uppercase labels where they add meaning.
- Carefully controlled text widths.
- Large amounts of intentional whitespace.
- Consistent vertical rhythm between sections.

Avoid oversized headings in every section. Establish a clear hierarchy and reserve the most dramatic typography for important moments.

Use a responsive layout with fluid typography, sensible maximum widths, and well-designed mobile spacing.

## 11. Technical implementation

First, inspect the existing project and understand its framework, package manager, routes, components, styling approach, assets, and available backend integrations.

Do not blindly replace the project's current architecture.

Work with the existing stack wherever possible.

If this is a fresh project, prefer the following when appropriate:
- React with TypeScript.
- Vite or the project's existing framework.
- Tailwind CSS if it is already installed or appropriate.
- Framer Motion or Motion for React for reusable interface animations.
- GSAP with ScrollTrigger if the cinematic, scroll-linked sequences genuinely benefit from it.
- Native CSS transforms and SVG for lightweight interactive visuals.

Do not install both major animation libraries without a clear technical reason. Keep the implementation maintainable.

For the hero and scrollytelling, define animation states and timelines deliberately. Avoid dozens of unrelated scroll event listeners.

Use a clean component structure, for example:

- `Navbar`
- `HeroSection`
- `ProductTransformation`
- `InteractiveProductStudio`
- `ChannelShowcase`
- `VideoAdShowcase`
- `BrandVoiceDemo`
- `HowItWorks`
- `FinalCTA`
- `Footer`

Create reusable components for product previews, platform selectors, tone controls, and generated-content tabs.

All visible controls must do what they say.

Implement working form controls, upload states, navigation, tabs, buttons, platform selection, tone selection, copy actions, and playback controls.

Use semantic HTML, accessible keyboard interactions, appropriate focus states, and descriptive labels.

Optimize images, lazy-load below-the-fold assets, and prevent large layout shifts.

Do not add artificial delays to simulate intelligence. Do not hardcode fake metrics, fake customer testimonials, fabricated customer logos, or unverified claims.

## 12. Mobile responsiveness

Treat mobile as a first-class experience, not a scaled-down desktop.

On mobile:
- Preserve the visual identity and strong typography.
- Simplify or disable expensive parallax effects.
- Convert the floating hero scene into a readable, beautifully layered composition.
- Adapt the cinematic zoom sequences to fit small screens.
- Keep the product studio usable without horizontal scrolling.
- Make tabs and selectors touch-friendly.
- Ensure the entire product image and generated outputs remain visible.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Preserve the site's performance on average mobile hardware.

No clipped content, overlapping controls, accidental horizontal overflow, or unnecessarily tall dead spaces.

## 13. Performance and quality requirements

A spectacular website that stutters or breaks on mobile is not a successful result.

Prioritize:
- Smooth animations.
- Responsive image sizing.
- Minimal JavaScript overhead.
- Stable layouts.
- Accessible color contrast.
- Good keyboard navigation.
- Clean browser console output.
- Sensible component boundaries.
- Reduced-motion support.
- Working primary flows.
- Reliable error and loading states.

Avoid adding WebGL or a heavyweight 3D engine unless it creates a clear improvement that cannot be achieved with simpler methods.

Test the actual page at desktop, tablet, and mobile widths.

Verify that the key animations work, the hero renders correctly, the product-demo controls respond, copy actions work, and no section causes horizontal overflow.

Fix any implementation issues discovered during testing.

## 14. Design details that distinguish exceptional work

Pay particular attention to details that are easy to overlook:

- The transition from the hero into the first scroll section must feel intentional.
- The scroll-zoom sequence must stay visually aligned with the viewport.
- Floating elements should have a clear hierarchy and believable depth.
- Text must remain readable over backgrounds and images.
- Scroll animations should not leave content invisible if a script fails.
- Images must have suitable crops and aspect ratios.
- Buttons should have a consistent visual language.
- The product UI should resemble a cohesive application, not unrelated mockup fragments.
- Every section should provide a reason to continue scrolling.
- The final design should look excellent even when every animation is disabled.

The end result should have a memorable opening, a surprising visual journey, a convincing interactive product demo, and a clear conversion path.
