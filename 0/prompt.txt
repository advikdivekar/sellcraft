# 6. MOTION DESIGN RULES

The motion quality must be exceptional.

Treat every animation as a designed movement with an explicit starting point, trajectory, acceleration, deceleration, and resting state.

## Motion principles

### A. Use continuous spatial logic

When an element moves between two positions, it must travel along a coherent path.

Avoid arbitrary cuts between unrelated scales or positions.

When transitioning between scenes, find relationships between the outgoing and incoming compositions.

For example, a product image can become the hero image inside the application, which can then become one tile in the finished marketing kit.

The audience should feel that the story evolves continuously rather than restarting with each scene.

### B. Use sophisticated easing

Use carefully chosen easing functions.

Favor smooth, controlled cubic Bézier curves and spring dynamics only where they genuinely improve the motion.

A useful starting point for many UI and headline transitions is a cubic Bézier similar to:

`cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)`

Do not apply one easing function indiscriminately to every animation.

Use different timing characteristics for:
- Editorial headline reveals.
- Camera pushes.
- UI navigation.
- Product-card entrances.
- Background transitions.
- Tiny interface details.

Avoid default CSS ease, abrupt linear starts, overshoot-heavy bounce, excessive elastic movement, and uncontrolled spring wobble.

If a spring is used, tune its damping and settle time carefully.

### C. Animate with precision

Use animation durations appropriate to the function:

- UI state transitions: approximately 180–350 ms.
- Small micro-interactions: approximately 120–220 ms.
- Headline reveals: approximately 450–900 ms.
- Major composition transitions: approximately 650–1,200 ms.
- Slow cinematic pushes: adjusted to the shot duration.

These are initial guidelines, not rigid limits.

Use overlapping animations to create fluidity. A camera move, mask reveal, text entrance, and sound cue can cooperate rather than execute one after another.

Avoid moving too many independent elements at once.

### D. Use parallax sparingly

Use restrained layered depth in hero and brand scenes.

A foreground product, middle-ground interface, and background gradient may move at slightly different rates to produce subtle dimensionality.

Depth should increase visual richness, not make the product feel like an interactive game menu.

Do not apply exaggerated 3D rotations to flat UI screenshots.

### E. Make typography part of the motion

Text should be animated using layout, masking, and controlled movement rather than simple fade-ins for every scene.

Consider:
- Text revealed through a clipping mask.
- Headlines moving a small distance into their final position.
- Tracking that settles during a logo reveal.
- A short word appearing as the preceding word resolves.
- Small labels entering with precise stagger timing.
- A headline changing through an intentional composition transition.

Do not animate entire paragraphs word by word.

The final state of every headline must remain sharp, readable, and stable.

### F. Avoid motion-design clichés

Do not use:
- Endless floating glass cards.
- Random particles everywhere.
- Generic purple AI gradients.
- Repeated spinning 3D cubes.
- Constant glowing borders.
- Unnecessary glitch effects.
- Artificial camera shake.
- Excessive bloom.
- Oversized cursors.
- Continuous parallax in every scene.
- Random UI panels with meaningless values.

Use effects only where they communicate product behavior or reinforce the story.
